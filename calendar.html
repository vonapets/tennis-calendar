Which tennis tournaments to list, in what order, every week to 31 December 2026 · built

How to read the numbers. Every projection is what the tournament traded on Polymarket (own history × 3–8× platform growth) or Kalshi, whichever is noted. Those are the two largest venues in the sport and they are useful for ranking tournaments against each other. They are not a forecast of what any other book will do — a newer or smaller venue should read the ordering, not the levels.

Where a row is marked [E] none the event has never traded anywhere and the number is a regional prior, not a measurement. That applies to every slot 1 pick below.

ITF — why it is not in slot 1, and when it should be

ITF is the largest sub-tour pool on Kalshi at $3.93bn across 237 tournaments, a third of all its per-match tennis. It is absent from the grid above only because nothing publishes dated ITF draws. That absence is a data gap, not a verdict.

Order of operations

  1. Mon 21 Sep — start the Challenger roll with San Diego. Then never miss a week. The point is not the volume of any one event; it is that the tennis card becomes always-on, with no week where there is nothing to trade.
  2. Mon 5 Oct — open slot 2 with Shanghai Masters, the largest draw available before the Australian Open (96, two weeks).
  3. Mon 26 Oct — Basel. Roughly equal to Shanghai on projection with a third of the draw.
  4. Mon 2 Nov and Sun 15 Nov — Paris Masters and the ATP Finals (the Finals run 15–22 Nov, so they straddle the week boundary below). Plan these in advance and hold the slot. They are the exam, they come once a year, and the ATP Finals needs only 18 markets.
  5. Set a go/no-go for late October. Decide in advance what a listed tournament has to clear for the plan to be working. If the weekly Challenger card is not meeting it by then, listing Paris will not fix that, and the problem is upstream of tournament selection.
